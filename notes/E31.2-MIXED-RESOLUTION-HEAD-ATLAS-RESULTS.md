# E31.2 mixed-resolution head atlas — results

Date: 2026-08-21

## Bottom line

The preregistered all-gates claim failed, but the experiment found strong,
fresh-prompt evidence that the **magnitude** of three coarse attention-block
interactions is concentrated in very few heads.

A discovery-frozen set of 48 out of 432 measured head pairs (three 4-by-4
rectangles) captured **93.8%** of the interaction energy on 32 untouched
confirmation prompts. It recovered **97.9%** as much energy as the best
rectangles selected with hindsight on confirmation, and the three complete
12-by-12 energy maps transferred with Spearman correlations of
`0.818–0.910`.

The strict result is nevertheless **fail**, because two secondary requirements
did not pass. Exact signed effects replicated for only `10/16`, `11/16`, and
`16/16` selected edges across the three parents, rather than at least `12/16`
in every parent. More importantly, interaction-selected head groups beat an
equally sized singleton-importance baseline in direct reconstruction for only
one of three parents, rather than two. The experiment therefore supports
sparse interaction magnitude, but not yet an interaction-specific discovery
advantage or a fixed signed edge graph.

## Frozen experiment

- Model and behavior: one pinned GPT-2-small checkpoint on directed IOI.
- Parent attention-block pairs: L5×L8, L8×L9, and L5×L10.
- Parent selection was explicitly post-hoc to the full E31.1 V2 result; all
  E31.2 head outcomes were prospective.
- For each parent and split, four response-blind k10, layer-0-MLP-off coarse
  backgrounds were used.
- Every 12×12 cross-layer head pair was measured by the exact four-cell raw
  conditional second difference `R11-R10-R01+R00`.
- Sixteen discovery contexts selected an exact maximum-energy 4×4 Cartesian
  rectangle for each parent. Thirty-two disjoint confirmation contexts tested
  those fixed rectangles.
- A separate direct intervention patched each selected four-head group as a
  group and compared its measured second difference with the all-12-head
  parent interaction. No pair-coefficient sum or affine calibration was used.
- The pipeline executed exactly **101,504** live-counted model examples, with
  all 48 clean contexts screened once, no duplicated clean forwards, frozen
  discovery geometry before confirmation authorization, and exact geometry
  replay before scoring.

## Registered result

Seven of nine registered checks passed:

- Aggregate confirmation energy coverage: **0.9382** (required `>=0.50`).
- Fraction of confirmation-oracle rectangle coverage: **0.9792** (required
  `>=0.75`).
- One-sided 95% bootstrap lower bound on coverage: **0.9243** (required
  `>=0.40`).
- The selected support beat the 95th percentile of 10,000 jointly matched
  random rectangle triples: **0.9382** versus **0.4264**.
- Full-map energy Spearman correlations were **0.8823**, **0.8181**, and
  **0.9099**, all above `0.50`.
- Direct group reconstruction reached pooled **R2=0.8617** and Pearson
  **0.9642**, above `0.50/0.70`.

Two checks failed:

- Exact sign replication was `10/16`, `11/16`, and `16/16`; every parent was
  required to reach at least `12/16`.
- Pair-selected reconstruction beat singleton-selected reconstruction in only
  `1/3` parents; `2/3` were required. The singleton baseline was better
  overall (`R2=0.9074`, MSE `0.00222`) than pair selection (`R2=0.8617`, MSE
  `0.00331`).

Because every gate was required, the primary verdict is **fail**.

## Post-score diagnostics

These diagnostics were not registered gates and do not change the fail:

- Only **2**, **4**, and **6** of 144 head pairs were needed to contain 90% of
  confirmation energy for L5×L8, L8×L9, and L5×L10. The corresponding
  inverse-Simpson effective supports were `1.57`, `4.01`, and `3.07`.
- The leading pair alone carried `78.4%`, `35.2%`, and `52.8%` of total energy
  in the three parents.
- The confirmation energy matrices were almost separable by endpoint-head
  strength: their leading singular component explained more than `99.7%` of
  squared matrix norm in every parent. Signed-mean matrices were similarly
  close to rank one (`>99.2%`).
- In the two parents that failed the unweighted sign-count gate, the flipped
  edges carried only **2.83%** and **0.24%** of selected confirmation energy.
  Thus dominant interactions retained sign while low-energy rectangle fringe
  edges flipped.
- Discovery top-four arbitrary edges recovered `95.3%`, `95.9%`, and `81.0%`
  of fresh energy, close to the confirmation-oracle top-four values of
  `96.3%`, `95.9%`, and `83.7%`.

The most economical current description is therefore not an arbitrary sparse
edge graph. It is a **node-sparse, nearly separable interaction backbone**:
few heads on each side produce almost all measured nonadditivity. This also
explains why singleton-selected groups reconstructed the coarse interaction at
least as well overall as pair-selected groups.

## Scientific interpretation

Strongly supported:

- Conditional head-pair interaction magnitude is extremely concentrated
  inside the three tested attention-block pairs.
- The magnitude structure transfers to fresh prompts and beats matched random
  rectangles by a wide margin.
- Small directly patched head groups reproduce most of the whole-block
  interaction without fitted calibration.

Not supported:

- A fixed signed 4×4 interaction graph.
- Superiority of pair-interaction ranking over ordinary singleton-head
  importance for mechanism discovery.
- Direct communication, independent causal paths, or an orthogonal variance
  decomposition.
- Generality beyond this checkpoint, task, background regime, or the three
  post-hoc parent blocks.

## Recommended next step

Do not expand another fixed 4×4 graph or scale the model yet. First run a fresh,
equal-budget selector competition that distinguishes three hypotheses:

1. arbitrary sparse edges;
2. a node-sparse Cartesian backbone selected from singleton effects; and
3. a low-rank/separable interaction score selected from discovery only.

Allow support size to vary on discovery under a fixed query budget, then freeze
the selectors and compare fresh direct reconstruction, ablation/rescue, energy
coverage, and query efficiency. Use energy-weighted sign stability or a
predeclared high-energy core rather than counting near-zero fringe signs
equally. If pair-aware selection again fails to beat node/singleton selection,
the correct mesoscale abstraction is a sparse set of interacting **nodes** with
a nearly separable interaction law, not a sparse arbitrary edge graph.
