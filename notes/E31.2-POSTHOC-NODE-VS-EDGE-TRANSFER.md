# E31.2 post-hoc node-versus-edge transfer diagnostic

Status: **post-hoc development diagnostic only**. This analysis was designed
after the E31.2 confirmation results were visible. It makes no new prospective
claim and does not change the registered E31.2 FAIL. It used the sealed labels
only and made **zero model calls**.

## Question

E31.2 found that head-pair interaction magnitude was extremely concentrated and
nearly rank one. This diagnostic asks a narrower question: does a full 12-by-12
pair map carry confirmation-stable information beyond the importance of its two
endpoint heads?

For every parent and split, the target is the existing raw interaction energy
map,

`mean[(R11 - R10 - R01 + R00)^2]`,

averaged over contexts and the four registered covers. Four discovery-frozen
predictors are compared on confirmation:

1. the outer product of the two heads' singleton-effect energies, with one
   no-intercept scalar fitted on discovery;
2. the outer product of the full pair map's row and column energy totals;
3. the leading rank-one SVD term of the full pair map;
4. the raw discovery pair map.

The singleton outer product's **support ranking** needs only the endpoint
effects. Its reported R2 scale uses the one scalar fitted to the discovery pair
map; this scalar does not affect its energy ranking. Row/column, SVD, and raw
rankings all require the full discovery pair atlas and are therefore structural
diagnostics, not query-efficient selectors.

## Main results

Across all 432 pairs, the endpoint-only ranking transfers moderately well, but
the full-map node factors are clearly better at the smallest useful budgets:

| Discovery predictor | Confirmation R2 | Pearson | Spearman | top-3 coverage | top-6 coverage | top-12 coverage |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Singleton-effect outer | 0.652 | 0.836 | 0.852 | 0.453 | 0.489 | 0.858 |
| Row/column outer | **0.711** | **0.879** | **0.869** | **0.485** | **0.724** | **0.886** |
| Rank-one SVD | 0.699 | 0.876 | 0.863 | **0.485** | **0.724** | **0.886** |
| Raw pair map | 0.699 | 0.876 | 0.860 | **0.485** | **0.724** | **0.886** |

The full raw map gives essentially no energy-ranking advantage over its
row/column or rank-one approximation. After removing the split-local rank-one
energy map, the remaining squared norm is tiny:

| Parent | Discovery residual | Confirmation residual |
|---|---:|---:|
| L5 attention x L8 attention | 0.0033% | 0.0247% |
| L5 attention x L10 attention | 0.4272% | 0.0126% |
| L8 attention x L9 attention | 0.1955% | 0.2632% |

The residual no-calibration R2 values are -0.106, -3.876, and -1.986. Some
residual correlations or top-k coverages look nonzero, but they concern at most
0.43% of discovery and 0.27% of confirmation squared map norm and do not form a
robust cross-parent result.

## Signed-map check

Signed interaction means reveal why the ordinary singleton baseline sometimes
fails. The singleton-effect outer predictor has confirmation Pearson
correlations 0.944, 0.422, and 0.544 across the three parents, whereas the raw
signed pair maps reach 0.948, 0.950, and 0.871. Thus there is stable structure
beyond the measured singleton main effects in two parents.

However, that extra structure is itself almost entirely a different rank-one
endpoint factor:

| Parent | Discovery signed rank-one fit R2 | Confirmation Pearson: signed rank one / raw | Discovery / confirmation residual norm after rank one | Residual R2 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| L5 attention x L8 attention | 0.9933 | 0.945 / 0.948 | 0.662% / 0.659% | 0.451 |
| L5 attention x L10 attention | 0.9949 | 0.953 / 0.950 | 0.496% / 0.134% | -1.190 |
| L8 attention x L9 attention | 0.9724 | 0.872 / 0.871 | 2.690% / 0.725% | -7.638 |

Only L5-by-L8 has a somewhat stable residual (Pearson 0.717), but it represents
about 0.66% of signed-map squared norm and is a single post-hoc case. It is too
small and isolated to support an edge-specific claim.

## Bottom line

The strongest current interpretation is **stable sparse node-factor structure,
not a stable arbitrary sparse edge graph**. A full head-pair atlas mostly learns
one interaction-propensity number per head on each side; multiplying those two
numbers reconstructs nearly all pair magnitude. Ordinary singleton causal
effects do not recover those latent propensities reliably, so the remaining
practical problem is to estimate them cheaply—for example with a small number of
anchor pairs or adaptive rank-one matrix completion—and then test that estimator
prospectively.

This conclusion is limited to three post-hoc-selected attention parents in one
GPT-2-small IOI experiment. Interaction energy remains a conditional second
difference, not an orthogonal causal variance decomposition, and a selected pair
is not necessarily a direct communication edge.
