# E32.1 exact-ground-truth sparse interaction benchmark — results

Date: 2026-08-21

## Bottom line

The preregistered claim failed: raw two-component intervention interactions did
not localize the six known direct circuit edges better than the strongest
node-factor comparator on InterpBench case 97. Raw confirmation edge AUPRC was
`0.4899`, versus `0.5370` for the rank-one node factor, and raw recovered `2/6`
true edges in its top six versus `3/6` for the comparator. Only the registered
discovery-to-confirmation stability gate passed (`rho=0.9973`). Cases 2 and 21
were therefore not run.

The useful positive result is more specific. The pair map was extremely sparse,
highly reproducible, and strongly enriched for the circuit's **causal
reachability closure**. Its top ten contained exactly five direct edges and five
indirect-but-transitively-connected pairs, with no unrelated pair. However, a
rank-one endpoint model explained `98.75%` of the pair-score squared energy and
slightly improved direct-edge recovery. The experiment supports sparse causal
participation and reachability, not a sparse graph of irreducible direct
interactions.

## Frozen experiment

- Primary organism: pinned InterpBench case 97, a three-layer/four-head Tracr
  transformer implementing categorical scale-by-maximum.
- Internal universe: 15 whole attention-head/MLP nodes and 87
  topology-permitted directed candidate edges.
- Projected official ground truth: six nodes and six direct edges. The benchmark
  and case identity were explicitly inspected during method design; this was not
  a hidden-benchmark evaluation.
- Discovery and confirmation used 64 and 128 behavior-correct clean/source row
  pairs. All 384 token rows were unique, with zero cross-split overlap.
- Every empty, singleton, and topology-valid pair intervention was evaluated.
  The scientific response was the class-centered output-logit tensor at the nine
  non-BOS positions.
- Pair score was mean squared conditional second difference
  `J=Yij-Yi-Yj+Y0` across examples, positions, and classes.
- The run used exactly `26,592` model examples and 52 physical forward calls.

## Registered result

| Method | Confirmation edge AUPRC | AUROC | TP@6 | Node AUPRC | D/C Spearman |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Raw pair interaction | `0.4899` | `0.9486` | `2` | `0.9444` | `0.9973` |
| Rank-one node factor | **`0.5370`** | **`0.9588`** | **`3`** | `0.9444` | `0.9991` |
| Singleton endpoint product | `0.3922` | `0.9300` | `2` | **`0.9762`** | `0.9987` |
| Absolute pair residual | `0.2485` | `0.6420` | `2` | `0.9444` | `0.9866` |
| Positive pair residual | `0.0760` | `0.3210` | `0` | `0.9444` | `0.9693` |

Chance direct-edge AUPRC was `0.0690`. Raw was therefore informative but failed
two required superiority checks:

- its AUPRC margin over the best comparator was `-0.0471`, not at least `+0.05`;
- its TP@6 advantage was `-1`, not at least `+1`.

The stability requirement passed comfortably. The conjunctive primary verdict
is nevertheless **fail**, so the registered secondary and full-panel hypotheses
remain untested.

## What the pair map actually recovered

The confirmation raw map was concentrated:

- `K90=10/87` candidate edges;
- inverse-Simpson effective support `9.63`;
- the leading pair held `20.24%` of total energy;
- the discovery and confirmation top-6, top-10, and top-15 supports were
  identical.

Its top ten split exactly into five direct ground-truth edges and five
ground-truth-transitive nonedges. Against the post-hoc direct-plus-transitive
closure of 15 pairs, raw confirmation AUPRC was `0.8502` versus `0.1724` chance,
and precision at ten was `1.0`. Of total raw interaction energy, `41.20%` lay on
the six direct edges, `53.67%` on the nine transitive nonedges, and only `5.13%`
on the other 72 candidates.

This explains the direct-edge failure. Four of the raw top-six false positives
were transitive circuit pairs, not arbitrary noise. The leading pair was the
non-edge L0 MLP to L1 MLP, whose endpoints are connected by a true multistep
path. Co-patching compresses that path into an apparent long-range interaction.

## Node factor versus edge residual

A masked rank-one endpoint model explained `98.75%` of confirmation pair-score
squared energy (`R2=0.9859` after mean centering) and had exactly the same top-15
support as raw. It also beat raw on direct-edge AUPRC and TP@6. The cheaper
singleton endpoint product recovered five of six exact nodes in its top six and
had closure AUPRC `0.8848`, slightly above raw.

The residual tests do not rescue an edge-specific interpretation. Positive
residual AUPRC was only `0.0760` with zero true edges at K=6; absolute residual
AUPRC was `0.2485`. The consistently missed circuit node was a weak initial
attention head, while the other five circuit nodes were recovered strongly.

## Scientific interpretation

Supported:

- mesoscale intervention nonadditivity can be sparse and almost perfectly
  portable across fresh input panels;
- it can accurately identify active circuit nodes and their causal reachability
  closure;
- apparent shortcut edges can be structured consequences of true multistep
  computation rather than measurement noise.

Not supported:

- raw pair nonadditivity as a direct-edge estimator;
- stable, irreducible sparse pair residuals beyond node propensity;
- superiority over node-factor or singleton-based discovery;
- generalization beyond this one exact-ground-truth organism.

The working abstraction should therefore change from a sparse arbitrary edge
graph to a **sparse node set with a low-rank reachability map**. Pair
interventions remain useful for finding the active causal region, but direct
edges require a genuinely directed second stage such as edge/path patching,
EAP, or EAP-IG.

## Recommended next experiment

Run a prospectively registered node-first cascade:

1. use singleton interventions to shortlist nodes;
2. retain only topology-valid edges among those nodes;
3. apply directed edge/path patching and official EAP/EAP-IG on that shortlist;
4. compare direct-edge AUPRC, TP@K, recovered-subgraph faithfulness, and total
   model/gradient cost against exhaustive raw pairs and exhaustive EAP;
5. independently test the sparse/reachability/rank-one predictions on cases 2
   and 21 without reusing the failed E32.1 hierarchy.

If the cascade retains direct-edge recovery at substantially lower cost, it is
a useful empirical mechanism-discovery method. If pair residuals again add no
held-out lift, retire the sparse pair-specific interaction hypothesis and keep
the sparse-node/reachability result.
